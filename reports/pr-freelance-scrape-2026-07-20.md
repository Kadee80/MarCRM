## Market trends

**The fractional PR brief has settled into a recognisable shape: 1–2 days per month, six-month initial term, expansion tied to results.** Three of this week's four leads use almost identical structure. That's a meaningful shift from the "find us an agency" posture — buyers are now scoping senior individual operators in small, renewable increments. It rewards a consultant who can show results inside the first month, and it means a portfolio of four or five of these engagements is a realistic book rather than an aspiration.

**Buyers are explicitly pricing against agencies, and saying so in the brief.** The SJP posting's "contacts and judgement of an agency without the agency overhead" is the clearest articulation yet of the thesis behind this whole pipeline. This language is worth quoting back in pitches.

**Execution-only briefs are increasing.** The dysphagia posting is the second in three weeks where the buyer has already built strategy, media lists and campaigns and wants a freelancer purely to pitch. This is a distinct workstream from fractional leadership and probably deserves its own subvertical treatment in scoring — it converts faster and has a lower trust barrier, at the cost of lower day rates and less strategic anchoring.

**Sector fit remains the binding constraint, not volume.** The board has plenty of freelance PR work; almost none of it is in finance, legal or B2B. Three of the four leads this week are off-core-ICP on sector, and the one that isn't (SJP) requires UK market experience. This is the recurring pattern across recent runs — intent signals are healthy, sector fit is the bottleneck.

**Source concentration is becoming a risk.** Three of four leads came from a single board (The Work Crowd), which is UK-weighted. US-side sources (Indeed, ZipRecruiter, CareerBuilder, Mediabistro) returned almost entirely aggregator landing pages rather than individual dated postings this run. See caveat below.
