## Market trends

**Search returns are getting worse, not better.** This cycle produced an unusually high aggregator-to-posting ratio. Queries that a month ago surfaced named requisitions now return ZipRecruiter and Glassdoor category pages with salary-range headlines and no individual listings. Practical consequence: the yield per query is falling, and the leads that do surface increasingly come from niche boards (Idealist, The PR Net, Built In, Recruiterflow) rather than the majors.

**Crisis and reputation is the clearest paying demand.** RMC recruiting freelance crisis professionals in two markets simultaneously, plus the surrounding market data (freelance crisis management averaging ~$48/hr with a top quartile above $62), points to episodic-but-real demand. Crisis work suits freelance structurally — it is spiky, it needs seniority immediately, and no one staffs for peak.

**The fractional label has drifted away from comms.** Go Fractional lists 320 open roles, 49 of them marketing, and almost none are communications or PR. The fractional market has consolidated around engineering, finance and growth. Comms buyers are still using the words "consultant," "contract" and "freelance" rather than "fractional" — worth weighting the search clusters accordingly.

**Compensation signal is holding.** ZipRecruiter's freelance PR consultant benchmark sits at ~$135k annualised with most in the $150k–$170k band, and no softening is visible against prior cycles. The gap between that benchmark and briefs like Touch Health's $7,500 shows how wide the dispersion is between nonprofit and corporate buyers — worth being disciplined about which end Mark bids into.

**White-label continues to institutionalise.** 2026 pricing round-ups now publish standard entry points for white-label PR (roughly $350 local / $805 national at the high end, down to $99–$150 budget tiers), and the pitch to agencies leans on defined SLAs and partner-level QA. That is the competitive set for overflow work: Mark is selling senior judgment against a productised commodity, and the pitch should say so.
